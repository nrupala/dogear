Release log

Every build of this app, what changed, and where its release checks stand. We publish the state of a build honestly — including while checks are still running.

2026-11-07 — move around while you listen (web 1.7.0)

Status: released. This build adds paragraph navigation to the reader; it went live on the web on 2026-11-10, and the Android app follows through Google Play from 2026-11-12.

Start where you point. Every paragraph now carries plain buttons — Read from here and Read this only — and the reader controls gain previous/next paragraph buttons and a progress bar you can drag: let go and reading resumes at the nearest part. The spoken paragraph is highlighted and kept in view as reading moves. Your place is shown as a paragraph ("Your place: paragraph 4 of 12") and you can move the pin before you resume.

Your choice of reading voice. A voice picker on the reading screen lists DogEar's own chosen voice first, then your system default and the other voices on this device; your choice is remembered, and switching voice mid-document keeps your place.

Talk starts warm. When a voice model is on this device, the speech engine now starts in the background, so tapping Talk begins listening at once instead of waiting for the model to load. And on the reading screen, under Talk to DogEar, you can optionally name your own answer model (an endpoint and key of yours): questions are then answered in composed words from the passages DogEar finds, instead of a quotation. Your key stays on this device and is sent only to the endpoint you name. Without it, answers stay quotations from the document, exactly as before.

The free hour is now daily. The free plan’s allowance is 1 hour of listening every day, resetting at midnight — it was 10 hours a month until today (2026-11-07). A daily hour matches how DogEar is actually used now that you can talk with it: a companion you return to every day gets a daily allowance. The counting is unchanged — by the document, not the clock, re-listening free, a document you have started can always be finished — and the one-time unlock still removes the limit for good.

2026-10-24 — the voice learns to finish (the 1.1.0 release, web 1.6.0 — in development)

Status: in development. This build is being built and exercised as the DogEar 1.1.0 release; it is not a web release yet, and web readers stay on the current build until it is approved.

The app talks about itself, finishes what it starts saying, and explains its failures. Ask DogEar what it is, what the buttons do, how Talk works, about the voice models, or what the unlock includes, and it answers from its own built-in description. Answers are now spoken by a completing speaker: one piece at a time, each confirmed finished, with anything the speech engine drops recovered or honestly reported — never a silent stop mid-sentence. And when listening fails, DogEar now says why, in words, out loud: what happened and what to do about it.

2026-10-22 — the player becomes part of the app (1.5.0, Android beta.5)

Status: in testing (Android beta). This build is being exercised as the Android beta.5 candidate; it is not a web release. Web readers stay on 1.4.0 until this build is approved.

One player, owned by the app itself. The owner’s phone test of beta.4 found the Talk button and the pause/stop controls unreliable exactly while the app was speaking. The cause was structural: the controls lived in the web page, on the same thread as the voice engine’s work, and playback state lived in three places at once. In this build the Android app owns the player: a standing native control bar (Pause, Stop, Talk, Continue, and Save place, Bookmark, Favorite, Tag and Note — all usable while a document is being read aloud), one native state machine every surface (the bar, the lock screen, the page) commands, native microphone capture that opens only after the voice has stopped, and the reading place kept by the app itself, so Continue resumes exactly where you stopped. The page remains the library and the document; it reflects the player instead of being the player. The one honest limit: the larger voice models still wait for the stronger engine, exactly as 1.4.0 states.

2026-10-22 — the phone fixes: microphone, controls, models (1.4.0)

Status: release candidate. This build is a release candidate since 2026-10-22 — it ships as a release candidate on deploy and graduates after a clean week under watch, the same convention as the builds below.

Four fixes from a real phone test. The owner’s own phone test of the Android beta found four defects; each was pinned to its cause before it was fixed. The microphone now opens in the Android app — a missing system permission meant Talk could never start, and the error message wrongly blamed the listener’s own permission setting; error messages now name their real cause. The top row of controls is tappable again — it had been painted under the phone’s status bar, where taps never reached it. Voice models the in-app engine cannot safely run are no longer offered for download: the larger models downloaded fully and then could never load; the shelf now says so plainly instead, and they return when the stronger engine lands. And the reader offers a one-time battery exemption, because some phones put the app to sleep and stop background reading; background reading itself is re-proven on the shipped package — minimized and with the screen off, the place keeps advancing.

2026-10-22 — voice models become a choice (1.3.0)

Status: release candidate. This build is a release candidate since 2026-10-22 — it ships as a release candidate on deploy and graduates after a clean week under watch, the same convention as the builds below.

Your choice of voice model. Two-way voice no longer comes as one fixed model. The reading screen now offers a shelf of speech-recognition models — Tiny, Base, Small, Medium and Large — and you pick the one you want. Each model is shown with its size and its own licence before download; nothing is bundled with the app, and the shared runtime that runs the models downloads once, alongside the first model you choose. You can keep more than one model on the device, switch which one is in use, and remove any of them from the same shelf. Speech is still recognized entirely on your device, your voice is never uploaded, and answers to questions are still quotations from your document’s own words — only the choice is new; reading, the listening plan and everything else are unchanged.

One-time re-download. If you had the earlier (v1) voice pack on this device, the pack’s cache format has changed, so the old copy is cleared on upgrade and the picker offers a fresh download of whichever model you choose. That download happens once, as before.

2026-10-22 — the listening plan, document files, and two-way voice

Status: release candidate. This build is a release candidate since 2026-10-22 — it graduates on 2026-10-29 after a clean week under watch. Gate verdict: PASS WITH FINDINGS (our release gate; the findings and the checks still outstanding are recorded with the verdict, not hidden).

The listening plan. DogEar now carries the plan the Play Store release will carry: 1 hour of listening free every day (the allowance was monthly when this entry was written; it became daily on 2026-11-07 — see the newest entry), counted by the document — hearing a passage again costs nothing, a document you have started can always be finished, and nothing you brought is ever locked away. Beyond the free hours, a one-time unlock (US$24.99 in the Play app; no subscription, ever) removes the limit, and the voice pack below is included in it — not a separate charge. A licence key lifts the limit on a device the same way. The listening count is kept on your device only. This build also adds an About page with a tip box and a form for asking us to fix or add something — requests people send shape what gets built next.

Document files. File reading beyond plain text: PDF, EPUB e-books, Word (.docx) and HTML files can be added the same way .txt and .md files always could. The reading tools are carried inside the app itself and files are opened on your device — nothing is uploaded for conversion. Those document formats are unlocked by the unlock or a DogEar licence key, which you paste once; the app checks the key’s signature itself, on your device. Paste, links, .txt and .md stay free and unchanged, and the reading engine is the same pinned reader-core as before.

Two-way voice. The optional voice pack: a speech-recognition model (Whisper base, English, about 109 MB) that an unlocked device can download once, from inside the app, and that then runs entirely on the device. With it, you can talk to the reader — pause, resume, go back, start over — and ask questions about the document you have open. Answers are read aloud straight from the document’s own words: DogEar quotes, it does not make answers up, and when the document doesn’t answer a question it says so. No audio is uploaded, ever; there is no transcription service. The pack can be removed from the same place it was downloaded, and without it the app is exactly the app it was before.

Android pairing. This build also carries the bridge the Android app (v1.1.0 beta) uses to keep reading while the app is minimized or the screen is off — the two halves are live together from this build.

2026-10-16 — the speech fix, second round

The gate re-checked the fix described below in a real browser and the silence was still there — so that fix was not good enough, and we say so here. Instrumenting a real browser showed why: with no working voice, every sentence errors within milliseconds, the reader races through the whole document without speaking, and the failure is over before any timer can catch it. This build watches for speech itself — a sentence actually starting and finishing — instead of trusting the browser’s “speaking” flag: if listening produces no real speech, playback stops at once, your place is restored to exactly where you pressed Listen, and the warning appears. The check was proven in a real speechless browser, not only in simulated tests. The gate re-checks this build before its verdict can change.

2026-10-16 — gate results, and a speech fix

Our release gate has checked the three parts of this launch. Its verdicts, in its own terms:

  • reader-core v0.2.0 (the reading engine) — PASS WITH FINDINGS.
  • This web app — BLOCKED at the gate on a silent speech failure, described below. Fix in loop; re-gate to follow.
  • The installable app (PWA) — PASS WITH FINDINGS; install and offline proofs outstanding.

The block: in a browser that offers the speech feature but has no working voice, pressing Listen produced no sound and no warning — it failed silently. This build fixes that: if your browser has no usable voice, the app says so up front, in both the library and the reading view; and if a Listen press produces no speech within about two seconds, playback stops and the warning appears instead of silence. The link importer also now refuses two more spellings of private network addresses that it previously left to the platform to refuse, and a broken link in the app’s footer is fixed. The gate re-checks this build before its verdict can change.

2026-10-16 — the web app, first build

Status: release candidate. This build is live and in its one-week candidate window under review by our release gate. Gate verdicts: recorded in the entry above.

  • The DogEar web app: add a document by pasting text, uploading a .txt/.md/.html file, or fetching a public https link; hear it read aloud; your place is kept on this device and offered as a resume point when you return.
